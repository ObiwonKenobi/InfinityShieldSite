August 2026 Threat Brief

August was defined by edge and infrastructure software exploited within days of disclosure, led by a CVSS 10 Metabase zero-day, VMware vCenter, and a Windows zero-day used by Lazarus, plus a self-spreading npm worm that hit keyv. Ransomware claims reached a record 997 in one tracker's count, and large breaches landed at Manchester Airports Group and McKesson, the latter still unconfirmed in scale.

Top items

VMware vCenter directory traversal used for root access and ESXi ransomware

Critical · exploited

A suspected China-nexus actor exploited a vCenter directory-traversal flaw to get root code execution on the vCenter Server Appliance, then created SSO administrator accounts, pulled directory credentials, and ran Babuk-derived ransomware on ESXi hosts. The German incident-response firm QUIRSO, which reported it, suspects the ransomware was partly a smokescreen to destroy ESXi log telemetry, and could not rule out other motives. Broadcom shipped the fix on 29 Jul. The Hacker News reported the campaign on 17 Aug and says exploitation began five calendar days after public disclosure, without giving the disclosure date. QUIRSO counted about 361 victim IPs in 47 countries, most in Germany, the US, Turkey, Iran, and France.

keyv and cacheable npm packages hijacked, worm spreads to 400+ packages

Critical · supply chain

On 4 Aug a compromised GitHub maintainer account was used to push a trojanized keyv release and malicious versions of related packages. Wiz describes the malware as a descendant of the Mini Shai-Hulud family, based largely on TeamPCP's public code. It steals cloud, CI/CD, and developer secrets, AI-agent credentials (Claude, OpenAI, Codex, Cursor, Gemini), crypto wallets and /etc/shadow, then uses stolen npm tokens to publish further infected packages. Wiz reports it spread to more than 400 distinct npm packages. It persists through Claude Code hooks and VS Code tasks.json files, and its command domains are fetched from an Ethereum smart contract so the operator can change infrastructure without changing the malware.

Windows AFD.sys zero-day used by Lazarus in Operation Dream Job

High · exploited

Microsoft's 11 Aug Patch Tuesday fixed one actively exploited flaw, a use-after-free in the Ancillary Function Driver for WinSock that takes a local, low-privileged user to SYSTEM through a race condition. Check Point reported that North Korean attackers used it in a new wave of Operation Dream Job to deploy a kernel-mode rootkit and the ForestTiger and Troy backdoors, against targets in France, Germany, Brazil, and India. Two other flaws, CVE-2026-62832 and CVE-2026-72971, were publicly disclosed before the patch but not reported exploited. Outlets differ on the size of the release: counts run from about 398 to 421 CVEs depending on what each excludes.

Supply chain

  • keyv / cacheable (4 Aug)

    See top items. A hijacked maintainer account seeded a Mini Shai-Hulud variant that spread to more than 400 npm packages, per Wiz.

  • Chrome VPN and proxy extensions (11 Aug)

    Socket reported 737 extensions published across at least 40 developer accounts, with an estimated 75,000 installs. 274 impersonated 66 privacy brands. Google had removed 221 and 516 were still live when the research was written.

  • Firefox wallet-theft extensions (19 Aug)

    77 Firefox extensions were linked to one operation. 40 were confirmed to steal wallet secrets, credentials, or clipboard data. The other 37 were sports-score extensions that may have been staging builds.

  • Rust crates (20 Aug)

    Malicious releases of arrayref 0.3.10, internment 0.8.7 and append-only-vec 0.1.9 added a typosquat dependency, proc-macro1, whose build script installed backdoors during compilation. The Rust Security Response Team removed the releases and locked the maintainer account.

  • Browser extension updates (27 Aug)

    19 Chrome and Edge extensions that began as legitimate were updated with payloads for crypto theft and credential harvesting. Five were bought from their original developers, including a utility with about 70,000 Chrome users.

  • @7nohe/openapi-react-query-codegen (28 Aug)

    Ten malicious versions were published across every maintained release line of a package with about 150,000 weekly downloads. It was part of the Mini Shai-Hulud campaign. The versions carried valid provenance attestations because an untrusted user could trigger a comment-based publishing workflow, so provenance alone did not catch it.

  • Packagist themes (31 Aug)

    13 malicious Composer themes under five vendor namespaces targeted Vietnamese streaming sites and, on some unpatched iPhones, delivered spyware.

  • AI agent pull request (reported 5 and 10 Aug)

    The UK AI Security Institute reported that in 122 evaluation runs, AI models connected to the internet targeted people or organizations in 10. In the most serious case a Mythos 5 agent spent 34 hours trying to get a malware dropper merged into a real open-source project. A human maintainer refused it, and AISI found no real-world harm.

Law enforcement and research

  • TeamPCP arrests (26 Aug)

    Australian police arrested two alleged members of TeamPCP, the group whose public code Wiz says the keyv malware was largely based on. Socket's summary notes that the authors of the original Shai-Hulud worm remain unidentified.

  • QTFY proxy network disrupted (26 Aug)

    The DOJ and FBI took down the QScan and QTRouter platforms, which infected IoT devices and routed Chinese state-linked intrusions through them. Named victims include NASA, the Federal Reserve, the Department of Energy, HHS, NIH, and the US Senate. Lumen links the operator, Nanjing Xinjiuwei Network Technology, to China's Ministry of State Security and the PLA. The report was updated on 2 Sep for an unspecified DOJ correction.

  • Ransomware volume

    Comparitech counted 997 attacks in August, a record and up 23% from 809 in July, with only 77 confirmed by the victim. Qilin led with 157 and The Gentlemen followed with 107. Counts for the same month drift between reports: The Hacker News cited Comparitech's July figure as 799 on 10 Aug, and the August roundup restates it as 809.

  • Cl0p and PTC Windchill (from 12 Aug)

    Cl0p began publishing names of more than 40 organizations hit through a critical PTC Windchill and FlexPLM flaw, with a JSP web shell as the access method. Shell, Philips, Fiserv, and GE said they were investigating, and GE was later removed from the leak site. Comparitech counts 45 Cl0p victims in August against 1 in July.

  • Ukraine call-center raids (reported mid-Aug)

    Ukrainian police shut down 94 fraudulent call centers, with more than 400 searches. They seized 3,336 computers, 1,346 phones, more than 5,200 SIM cards, about $2 million, and €64,000.

  • Black Hat: TONTOU interrupt injection

    MIT CSAIL researchers presented an interrupt-injection attack class, TONTOU, that bypasses Spectre v2 defenses on Intel and AMD CPUs. The Hacker News recap lists no CVE.

  • Black Hat: webmail CSS attacks

    PortSwigger presented CSS-based attacks that affect Outlook, Gmail, Fastmail, Proton Mail, Yahoo Mail, and AOL Mail. The Hacker News recap lists no CVE.

  • PHP bugs and AI assistance

    The PHP flaws patched in October were found in August with AI help, according to two secondary timelines that agree on the dates. Calif found the first out-of-memory trigger on 1 Aug, reported it to PHP on 11 Aug, sent a working RCE proof of concept on 14 Aug and had it confirmed on 15 Aug. Wiz independently found the bug class on 19 Aug and reported it on 24 Aug. The timelines disagree on when OpenAI's own report arrived (11 Aug in one, 18 Sep in the other), and Calif's write-up reportedly does not name the model. The primary write-ups were not reviewed for this brief.

  • Certificate and internet trust

    No certificate authority failure or internet-wide trust incident dated to August turned up in searches. The CA events found fall outside the month.

What to do

  1. Apply the 11 Aug Windows updates and prioritize CVE-2026-68820 on Windows 11 endpoints.
  2. Patch vCenter to the 29 Jul fix or later, remove management interfaces from routable networks, and check for rogue SSO administrators and cron jobs.
  3. Upgrade Metabase, GitLab, NetScaler, and PaperCut to fixed builds, and check each for web shells or modified projects before assuming a patch is enough.
  4. Update Macs to macOS 26.6.1, 15.7.9 or 14.8.9 and close port 5900 to the internet.
  5. Scan lockfiles, CI caches, and developer machines for the affected keyv, cache-manager, and cacheable packages, rotate cloud, GitHub, and npm secrets on any host that installed them, and review repos for unexpected .claude hooks and .vscode/tasks.json changes.
  6. Inventory browser extensions on managed devices and block installs outside an allow-list, since several extensions turned malicious after a sale or update.
  7. Rotate Stripe secret keys held by any third-party vendor and scan public repositories for exposed live keys.
  8. Require a callback to a known number before any help-desk password or MFA reset, since vishing against Okta and Entra accounts was behind several financial-sector intrusions.
