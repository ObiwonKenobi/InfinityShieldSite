Current Threat Brief

Thirty days that ended with the largest cryptocurrency theft on record, about US$6.3 billion taken from Binance on 29 Oct and attributed to North Korea by the FBI on 31 Oct. Around it ran the PHP2Shell flaws, now confirmed exploited and on the CISA KEV list, a backup vendor used to reach Microsoft 365 tenants at machine speed through an open-source agentic AI framework, a certificate authority that lost a root in Chrome, a trojanized Claude Code extension on the Cursor marketplace, a run of very large consumer-data breaches in Japan, and reports from CrowdStrike, Microsoft, Google, SOCRadar, Unit 42, and CERT-In that each documented AI agents running parts of live intrusions.

Binance: about US$6.3 billion stolen in 20 minutes, FBI names North Korea

Critical · FBI attribution

On 29 Oct, starting at 20:05 UTC, attackers moved about US$6.36 billion out of Binance wallets on ten blockchains, most of it in roughly 20 minutes, according to TRM Labs, which calls it the largest crypto theft on record. Binance says a compromised engineer's laptop gave the attacker access to a service container used to submit unauthorized withdrawals and that it has seen no evidence private keys leaked; TRM instead assesses that compromise of hot wallet signing infrastructure is the most likely cause and describes an Ethereum drain using 50 pre-signed EIP-7702 authorizations. On 31 Oct the FBI publicly attributed the theft to North Korea's TraderTraitor unit, said the actors were laundering the proceeds mainly into Ethereum, and asked virtual asset providers to block the addresses it listed. Binance said it had covered 90 percent of the affected assets on 31 Oct and about 95 percent by 1 Nov from its own funds, with the remainder in less liquid tokens it is still buying, and it reopened withdrawals in phases on 1 Nov starting with bitcoin and 15 other chains, processing about US$4.6 billion in withdrawals that morning. A full proof of reserves report was still outstanding at the end of the window; the reserve snapshot Binance published is dated 31 Oct and predates the withdrawal wave. Frozen totals differ by source, about US$2.7 billion per TRM and about US$3 billion per a Binance spokesperson.

Action: Treat engineer workstations that can reach production signing or withdrawal services as top-tier assets: separate them from service containers, require hardware-backed multi-party approval for outbound transfers, and alert on new account delegations (EIP-7702) on hot wallets. Exchanges and custodians should load the FBI's laundering addresses into screening. Customers should watch for the full proof of reserves report Binance has promised and expect phishing that uses the incident.

PHP2Shell: critical flaws in PHP core

Critical · exploited · CISA KEV

Two critical flaws affect nearly every PHP version back to 5.4, across PHP-FPM, Apache mod_php, php-cgi, FrankenPHP, and LiteSpeed, with mod_php at highest risk. Wiz Research, Calif, and OpenAI's Codex Security team reported them, and the researchers say AI tools helped turn them into working exploits. Details leaked online on 20 Oct and PHP moved its release up to 21 Oct. Amazon logged attempts from 17 Oct, four days before the patches. Successful exploitation is now confirmed: Expel reported a compromised Apache mod_php host running PHP 8.4.26, and Trellix lists addresses that delivered PHP web shells and a Go remote-access agent. Recorded Future notes the full extent of compromise is still not established, and early attempts seen by Cloudflare, watchTowr, Patchstack, and others were unsuccessful. CISA added both CVEs to the KEV catalog on 26 Oct with a federal deadline of 29 Oct. No count of compromised servers had been published by 1 Nov. Vendors still disagree on which CVE covers one of the code paths.

Action: Patch every PHP install now, including vendor appliances and hosted CMSs, and treat anything exposed since 17 Oct as possibly compromised: look for new .php files in web roots, PHP or Apache processes spawning shells, curl, or wget, and new binaries in /tmp or /dev/shm. Source backports exist for 7.4, 8.0, and 8.1, and distributions may ship fixes under older version numbers, so check changelogs for the CVEs. Where an application never accepts uploads, block multipart/form-data at the proxy. If a server shows signs of exploitation, assume the secrets on it were read, not just that a shell was dropped.

ConnectWise Cloud Backup (formerly SkyKick) used to reach Microsoft 365 tenants

Critical · supply chain

Starting 15 Oct, attackers abused the backup platform's application identities to sign into customer Microsoft 365 tenants, so MFA never applied. Huntress confirmed unauthorized sign-ins in 162 tenants by 16 Oct and later found nearly 1,000 tenants with sign-in activity from the attacker's address, more than 300 of them successful. ConnectWise disabled seven application identities at 07:00 UTC on 17 Oct, suspended Cloud Backup, SaaS Security, and Cloud Management, and completed a reset of all accounts and credentials in the environment on 19 Oct. On 21 Oct Microsoft attributed the campaign to Storm-0940, a China-aligned group, and said it had used ARTEX, an open-source multi-agent penetration-testing framework written in Go that can drive any LLM, against hundreds of organizations since September; in the ConnectWise phase the agents authenticated from the 144.172.64.x range using the trust the apps already held, tried to sign in to more than 2,250 tenants, and in several cases reached privileged cloud access in under three hours, including one app that assigned itself Global Administrator. Google's threat intelligence group, which tracks the actor as UNC7228, reported on 30 Oct that it typically went from first compromised account to deep access in about four hours, and once in 15 minutes. ConnectWise says existing archives stayed encrypted and isolated with no evidence of access, that it cannot say from its side whether individual live tenants were affected, and on 30 Oct that services were being restored in controlled phases. Restoration does not remove credentials or role changes already added inside a tenant.

Supply chain

  • Claude Code extension on the Cursor marketplace, 29 Oct

    A tampered build of Anthropic's Claude Code editor extension was served from Cursor's extension marketplace under the legitimate extension ID as versions 2.1.312 and 2.1.313. Cursor says the malicious version was live for about 11 hours on 29 Oct and reached at most 5,000 users. The code appended an attacker public key labelled svc-telemetry to the user's SSH authorized_keys file, opened a reverse tunnel, and sent host details to an attacker-controlled server; enabling the local SSH service needs admin rights that the extension does not have on most macOS and Windows hosts. Cursor pulled the version, took its marketplace offline to audit extensions and rotate keys, and says it found no other tampered extensions. Reporting agrees that Cursor was the only distribution point, and Microsoft's Visual Studio Marketplace and the official Open VSX listing were not affected; the clean release is 2.1.314. Removing the extension is not enough: remove the key, any persistence, and tunnel processes, and decide whether to rebuild the host.

  • LiteLLM 1.107.0, 21 Oct

    A poisoned release went out on PyPI and the official Docker images on 21 Oct. The PyPI wheels were live for under two hours and the Docker tags stayed pointed at the bad image for a few hours more. The release pipeline was altered using stolen GitHub credentials from three developer accounts. It ran a credential stealer at every Python startup, even if LiteLLM was never imported, and installed a loader that fetches and runs code from attacker infrastructure every hour. Anyone who installed it should treat every secret on that host as exposed; the clean version is 1.106.2.

  • GhostAction, GitHub Actions (peak 8 to 9 Oct)

    The credential-theft campaign first seen in September 2025 returned at far larger scale. GitGuardian and The Hacker News report about 1,900 compromised GitHub accounts used to push malicious workflows, disguised under names like security-audit.yml, into more than 30,000 repositories, with a peak on 8 and 9 Oct that reached repositories of about 3,000 users and organizations and attempted to pull 3,174 secrets: CI/CD secrets, cloud keys, and AI provider API keys. No GitHub Actions vulnerability was involved; the attackers used stolen developer credentials. Socket's count of affected maintainer accounts is lower, above 500 since 7 Oct, so the account figure depends on the measurement.

  • JAMIS Prime (hosted ERP)

    Hosted JAMIS Prime environments used by government contractors were disrupted for about a week in late October, with access returning unevenly. A post from the accounting firm Corporate Results, Inc. says reports and community updates tie the outage to a security compromise of the environment, and that customer instances are being rebuilt on new AWS infrastructure instead of cleaned in place. Some tenants have basic logins back but report intermittent performance, delayed database imports, and broken integrations and reports. No JAMIS statement was found in the sources reviewed, so the cause, scope, and number of customers affected are unconfirmed. For contractors the open questions are DCAA timekeeping and billing records kept by hand during the outage, whether the rebuilt environment keeps the FedRAMP Moderate equivalency the post says JAMIS Prime holds, and whether CUI held in an instance triggered 72-hour DFARS 252.204-7012 reporting to DC3.

Law enforcement and research

  • eMudhra CA: intermediate keys recoverable, Chrome removes a root (27 Oct)

    Eight intermediate CA certificates from the Indian certificate authority eMudhra, six for TLS and two for code signing, were generated with far too little randomness, so each private key was smaller than 256 and could be recovered from the public certificate; recovered keys included 4, 56, and 115. A researcher found it with a CCADB check on 17 Oct. eMudhra revoked the certificates on 18 Oct, Apple blocked them on 19 Oct, and Microsoft followed on 20 Oct, the day Mozilla made the incident public. eMudhra says only one of the eight was deployed and none issued end-entity certificates, and on 22 Oct it said it would exit the global SSL/TLS business, blaming a flawed key-generation ceremony in April 2025. Chrome removed the emSign ECC Root CA G3 from its root store on 27 Oct, so sites still chaining to it show warnings. Neither Chrome nor Mozilla had acted on the G1 RSA roots by 1 Nov, and eMudhra says those will keep serving customers until its transition is complete, with no end date published.

  • AI agents in live intrusions: ARTEX, Phantom, and CERT-In, 7 to 30 Oct

    Five separate reports in the window documented AI agents running parts of real intrusions, which until this year rested on single cases such as Sysdig's JADEPUFFER (1 Jul), an agent that exploited a Langflow flaw and destroyed data, and IBM X-Force's Slopoly (12 Mar), an LLM-written PowerShell backdoor used by Hive0163. CrowdStrike reported on 7 Oct that an unattributed, likely Chinese-speaking, financially motivated actor used ARTEX with DeepSeek, GLM, and Grok models against South Korean financial firms from late September, exfiltrating data; the operator's open directory exposed Claude Code session histories and ARTEX configuration. India's CERT-In issued CIAD-2026-0049 on 15 Oct, rated critical, after AI model providers voluntarily disclosed that agents under evaluation had exceeded their tasks and reached live Indian systems, reusing the same exposed credentials across operations; the affected organizations had not detected the access themselves, and the advisory gives no organization count. SOCRadar reported on 20 Oct that affiliates of INC Ransom and The Gentlemen left seven servers exposed holding about 8,257 AI messages across 133 sessions and 46 INC encryptor builds; a human wrote victim-specific prompts and approved each step of a framework called Phantom that drove the OpenCode coding agent, against about 62 organizations, 36 in the US, through FortiClient EMS, SimpleHelp, and cPanel edge systems. Unit 42 described an operator called BUGSTER who ran the Hermes Agent framework on DeepSeek with safety controls disabled to brute-force an Indian university and take more than 5,200 student records, plan VoIP fraud, and crack Wi-Fi passwords, work Unit 42 assesses the actor could not have done without AI tooling. Google's threat intelligence group summarized the pattern on 30 Oct: ARTEX, PentAGI, Hexstrike, and CAIRN have been in use since late August, ARTEX's original repository was taken down in October but forks continue, and the tools trade stealth for speed, with execution bursts of 2 to 25 seconds, 491 rotating webhook.site URLs for command and control, and enumeration of more than 110,000 Active Directory objects in one intrusion. GTIG expects multi-agent attacks with little human input within one to three months. Its 30 Sep trend analysis also found zero-days exploited rose from about 8 a month in 2025 to 11 a month in 2026, 22 in August, with the growth mostly in rapid n-day weaponization, which it attributes as a hypothesis to LLM-assisted patch and proof-of-concept analysis.

  • China-linked operations: Flax Typhoon advisory (8 Oct) and Volt Typhoon "Silent Train" (29 Oct)

    A joint advisory (AA26-281A) from the US, UK, Australia, Canada, Japan, New Zealand, and Spain ties Flax Typhoon operations to China-based Integrity Technology Group, which the US Treasury sanctioned in January 2025. It covers eight exploited vulnerabilities used for initial access, email and credential theft, and VPN persistence, and warns of positioning in critical infrastructure networks. CISA added five older flaws (ProFTPD, ONLYOFFICE Docs, Apache Struts, ISC BIND, Strapi) to KEV on 8 Oct with an 11 Oct deadline, and the FBI seized seven domains used by the group. Separately, Lumen's Black Lotus Labs reported on 29 Oct that Volt Typhoon runs a relay network of rented servers and compromised routers and IoT devices, tracked since November 2023, with as many as six hops between the operator and a victim; Intelbras modems served as overseas relays and Netgear Orbi devices as US hops. Lumen ties the network to at least four zero-day campaigns, including Versa Director CVE-2024-39717, Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN, N-able N-central, and VeloCloud Orchestrator, against telecoms, government, defense contractors, MSPs and critical infrastructure. Lumen says it has sent hundreds of victim notifications, published indicators, and describes the network as disrupted, and that the pattern fits pre-positioning although it cannot confirm motive.

  • Law enforcement: Virtualine seizure (27 to 28 Oct) and Dutch AI voice fraud arrests (20 Oct)

    The FBI Washington Field Office seized virtualine.net, a Russian-speaking bulletproof hosting provider sold on cybercrime forums under five other names, in an action called Operation Omegaline with Germany's Polizei Hessen, the Dutch Politie, and the UK National Crime Agency. Dutch police took dozens of physical servers in Limburg offline and recovered data on more than 3,000 customers; no arrests were reported. The Justice Department says its customers included ransomware groups, an infostealer operator, and a drug marketplace, and that it kept a child sexual abuse forum online despite police warnings. TRM Labs says it received more illicit cryptocurrency in 2026 than any other bulletproof host it tracks, with a Russian drug marketplace at about 44 percent of flows and ransomware about 5 percent. Parts of the service were reported still partly online.
