Flock Camera Tracker

What the cameras record

A Flock license plate camera is a camera. It photographs every vehicle that passes, and the plate read is only the part that gets indexed first. Flock describes a "vehicle fingerprint" feature that also records the make, body type, color, decals, bumper stickers and roof racks, and whether a plate is missing or temporary, so a search can run on a description with no plate number (Flock’s own description). What else shows in an image depends on the camera’s angle and lighting.

Some Flock models are general video cameras, not plate readers. 404 Media reported in December 2025 that dozens of Flock’s Condor pan-tilt-zoom cameras, which can be set to zoom in on people, had live video streams and admin panels reachable from the open internet. The product line and its capabilities change, so treat this as a description of what has been reported, not a spec sheet.

How to read this

These are cameras volunteers have mapped, not an official inventory. A jump in the line usually means someone surveyed an area, so it tracks installation but does not measure it, and cameras that were never mapped are missing. Counts include only nodes tagged as Flock Safety by manufacturer or brand; the fourth tile counts every plate reader tagged in the box, whatever the maker.

Both areas are rectangles (south, west, north, east), not legal boundaries, so the county box takes in a sliver of neighbouring counties on its north and west edges. Laramie County (bounding box): 40.99, -105.28, 41.6, -104.05; Cheyenne area (bounding box): 41.07, -104.95, 41.24, -104.65. Earlier snapshots marked attic are rebuilt from OpenStreetMap edit history, so they show what was mapped by that date and nothing about the days before it was surveyed.
