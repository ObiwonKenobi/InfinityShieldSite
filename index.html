Top items

Binance: record US$6.3 billion theft traced to a fake job task and tampered download, US moves to seize US$1.2 billion

Critical · FBI attribution · seizure order

On 29 Oct attackers moved about US$6.3 billion out of Binance wallets on ten blockchains in roughly 20 minutes, the largest crypto theft on record (TRM Labs says US$6.36 billion, some outlets US$6.34 billion). The FBI attributed it to North Korea's TraderTraitor unit on 31 Oct, and North Korea denied it on 3 Nov, calling the accusation a "grave political provocation". Binance's 3 Nov post-mortem says the intrusion ran for months across three engineers' computers: a tampered Homebrew download in June, which Binance says it removed after the attackers had learned who managed wallets, and a fake job process in September in which an engineer was asked to download a coding task that carried malware, ending at the system that signs transactions. Binance says it replaced about 90 percent of the loss from company funds and reopened withdrawals in phases from 1 Nov. On 12 Nov the Justice Department announced court-authorized seizure or restraint of more than US$1.2 billion of the stolen funds, and Korea JoongAng Daily reported that this is part of more than US$2.7 billion frozen by US authorities and partners. TRM and Elliptic describe laundering through self-minted memecoins and chained bridges on Tron, NEAR, and Solana, and Chainalysis reports more than US$500 million moved through intermediary wallets on Tron. Elliptic notes the laundering has been slower than after the Bybit theft.

Action: Treat engineer workstations that can reach signing or withdrawal systems as top-tier assets: block tool installs from search ads, run unsolicited job-interview coding tasks only on disposable machines, and require hardware-backed multi-party approval for outbound transfers. Exchanges and custodians should screen against the FBI's address list and watch for newly minted tokens and bridge hops on Tron, NEAR, and Solana.

wfx-loot: 4,837 hijacked GitHub accounts used to plant a CI secret stealer

High · supply chain · still live on 12 Nov

Aikido Security named the campaign wfx-loot, reported on 12 Nov and run over about two days. Attackers used 4,837 hijacked GitHub accounts to open probe pull requests in 61,764 repositories and to push a shell script of roughly 1,500 lines that collects CI secrets and cloud, registry, and AI-service credentials, sends them to attacker servers, then reverts the commit and deletes the workflow run and its logs. Counts of affected repositories differ: Aikido says the payload ran in at least 1,835, other outlets say 1,806, and SafeDep reports 8,093 repositories that received malicious commits. No flaw in GitHub Actions was involved; the attackers used stolen developer credentials. Delivery was through edited Actions workflows, an npm install hook, or a new workflow file on a temporary branch. Confirmed malicious artifacts include a Packagist package and two Go module versions, and about 50 compromised accounts can publish to npm, Go, Packagist, PyPI, or crates, so follow-on publishing is the main risk. OX Security said on 12 Nov that payloads were still on the default branches of dozens of public repositories.

Action: Search every repository you own for workflows or commits added since 1 Nov that you did not write, treat any machine that ran npm install on an affected commit as compromised, rotate the CI, cloud, registry, and AI provider secrets your workflows can read, and require hardware-key MFA on GitHub accounts that can publish packages.

AI agents running live intrusions: FBI warns on ARTEX, CrowdStrike reports four actor clusters

High · AI-assisted intrusion

On 13 Nov the FBI warned that multiple threat actors have used the open-source ARTEX agentic penetration-testing framework since at least August 2026, with more than 1,600 publicly exposed ARTEX consoles identified and the true number believed to be higher. BleepingComputer's summary adds that a Chinese-speaking actor paired ARTEX with Claude Code agents against several South Korean banks; that detail was not checked against the FLASH itself. CrowdStrike reported on 4 Nov four actor clusters using agentic AI against healthcare, government, and enterprise targets; in one intrusion an agent moved laterally and harvested domain controller credentials in about 20 minutes without further human direction, and one Indonesian-speaking actor tasked Claude Code with more than 800 skills collected from public GitHub repositories. Check Point (2 Nov) described GOLD OGRE ransomware operators using AI agents and VAPOR PANDA using ARTEX against more than 200 organizations. Microsoft tied the ConnectWise Cloud Backup tenant intrusions to ARTEX on 21 Oct. These are vendor and advisory reports, and the speed figures come from the firms that measured them.

Supply chain

  • cPGuard update channel, 2 Nov

    Attackers used compromised credentials to reach developer OpsShield's update distribution network and pushed a malicious script as cPGuard versions 5.88.12 to 5.88.19. About 2,950 customer servers ran the fake update during roughly 70 minutes, and the vendor says it stopped distribution within 12 hours. The script ran as root, collected /etc/shadow and /etc/passwd, root database configuration, shell history, and SSH authorization and known-hosts files, and left a hidden root shell and a scheduled task. Update to 5.88.25 or later, then reset passwords and rotate database credentials, cloud secrets, and SSH keys, because the fix removes the backdoor but cannot invalidate what was already copied. How the credentials were stolen is still under investigation.

  • XCPE botnet in Android TV set-top boxes, disrupted 2 Nov

    Google's Threat Intelligence Group traced a botnet of more than 1.3 million set-top boxes to a backdoor built into the firmware at the factory by Shenzhen-based Videostrong Technology. Google and partners including Japan's National Police Agency, Lumen, HUMAN Security, Plume, Synthient, and TrendAI took down the command domains and servers, which Google says cuts access to at least 10 million additional compromised devices, and Google banned the company from making devices with preloaded Google apps. The malware is not removed from infected boxes and reinstalls itself from the /oem/ partition if deleted.

  • Claude Code extension on the Cursor marketplace, 29 Oct

    A tampered build of Anthropic's Claude Code editor extension was served from Cursor's extension marketplace under the legitimate extension ID as versions 2.1.312 and 2.1.313. Cursor says the malicious version was live for about 11 hours on 29 Oct and reached at most 5,000 users. The code appended an attacker public key labelled svc-telemetry to the user's SSH authorized_keys file, opened a reverse tunnel, and sent host details to an attacker-controlled server; enabling the local SSH service needs admin rights that the extension does not have on most macOS and Windows hosts. Cursor pulled the version, took its marketplace offline to audit extensions and rotate keys, and says it found no other tampered extensions. Reporting agrees that Cursor was the only distribution point, and Microsoft's Visual Studio Marketplace and the official Open VSX listing were not affected; the clean release is 2.1.314. Removing the extension is not enough: remove the key, any persistence, and tunnel processes, and decide whether to rebuild the host.

  • FaceStealer, North Korean social engineering aimed at open-source maintainers, 12 Nov

    The Justice Department and FBI announced on 12 Nov that they had seized cloud infrastructure behind a scheme the FBI calls FaceStealer, which it says has stolen more than US$400 million in cryptocurrency over the last 18 months. Attackers use accounts stolen from earlier victims to invite targets to video meetings, play recorded or deepfake video of people the target knows, then claim a technical error and offer a link that installs malware. The FBI says the scheme is now also aimed at people who develop and maintain open-source software and the repository accounts they control, which it says appears calculated to enable software supply chain attacks. The FBI seized more than 900 webcam videos of victims and published a report and a resource page at fbi.gov/facestealer. DOJ names Sapphire Sleet, UNC1069, BlueNoroff, and APT38 among the tracked actor names.

Law enforcement and research

  • Operation Endgame, 12 Nov

    Police in nine countries took down infrastructure behind the Gootloader, CastleRAT, and StealC malware families, which feed ransomware and credential theft. Europol reports 235 servers and 257 domains seized and credentials belonging to 6.4 million victims identified. The Justice Department says it removed Gootloader malware from more than 100 servers and links the tool to US$28.8 million in ransomware losses. One source reports no arrests had been announced as of 15 Nov, and the Justice Department named no defendant.

  • Volt Typhoon "Silent Train", 29 Oct

    Lumen's Black Lotus Labs reported on 29 Oct that Volt Typhoon runs a relay network of rented servers and compromised routers and IoT devices, tracked since November 2023, with as many as six hops between the operator and a victim; Intelbras modems served as overseas relays and Netgear Orbi devices as US hops. Lumen ties the network to at least four zero-day campaigns, including Versa Director CVE-2024-39717, Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN, N-able N-central, and VeloCloud Orchestrator, against telecoms, government, defense contractors, MSPs and critical infrastructure. Lumen says it has sent hundreds of victim notifications, published indicators, and describes the network as disrupted, and that the pattern fits pre-positioning although it cannot confirm motive.

  • Law enforcement: Virtualine seizure (27 to 28 Oct) and Dutch AI voice fraud arrests (20 Oct)

    The FBI Washington Field Office seized virtualine.net, a Russian-speaking bulletproof hosting provider sold on cybercrime forums under five other names, in an action called Operation Omegaline with Germany's Polizei Hessen, the Dutch Politie, and the UK National Crime Agency. Dutch police took dozens of physical servers in Limburg offline and recovered data on more than 3,000 customers; no arrests were reported. The Justice Department says its customers included ransomware groups, an infostealer operator, and a drug marketplace, and that it kept a child sexual abuse forum online despite police warnings. TRM Labs says it received more illicit cryptocurrency in 2026 than any other bulletproof host it tracks, with a Russian drug marketplace at about 44 percent of flows and ransomware about 5 percent. Parts of the service were reported still partly online. Separately, Dutch police announced on 20 Oct that the Rotterdam cybercrime team had arrested two men on 6 Oct, aged 23 from Vaassen and 25 from Ede, over a bank help-desk fraud that took about 380,000 euros from about 170 victims in three weeks. The gang used AI to generate dozens of audio fragments in different voices and bank scenarios for automated calls; victims who pressed 1 reached a human posing as bank staff who had them install an app and hold their bank card to the phone, which relayed the card's NFC data to an accomplice at a cash machine. The 23-year-old is suspected of building the technology and directing the group; the servers were taken offline and a court kept both in custody for at least 90 more days. Police in Nagpur, India, reported four arrests on 1 Nov over voice-clone and "digital arrest" frauds, but only local press has reported it so far.

  • eMudhra CA: intermediate keys recoverable, Chrome removes a root (27 Oct)

    Eight intermediate CA certificates from the Indian certificate authority eMudhra, six for TLS and two for code signing, were generated with far too little randomness, so each private key was smaller than 256 and could be recovered from the public certificate; recovered keys included 4, 56, and 115. A researcher found it with a CCADB check on 17 Oct. eMudhra revoked the certificates on 18 Oct, Apple blocked them on 19 Oct, and Microsoft followed on 20 Oct, the day Mozilla made the incident public. eMudhra says only one of the eight was deployed and none issued end-entity certificates, and on 22 Oct it said it would exit the global SSL/TLS business, blaming a flawed key-generation ceremony in April 2025. Chrome removed the emSign ECC Root CA G3 from its root store on 27 Oct, so sites still chaining to it show warnings. Neither Chrome nor Mozilla had acted on the G1 RSA roots by 1 Nov, and eMudhra says those will keep serving customers until its transition is complete, with no end date published. Status as of 1 Nov; not rechecked in this refresh.

  • Johnson Controls False Claims Act cybersecurity settlement, 13 Nov

    Johnson Controls and two subsidiaries agreed to pay US$5 million to resolve allegations that they violated the False Claims Act by not meeting cybersecurity requirements in federal contracts and subcontracts, covering January 2018 to August 2025 and work for NASA and the Departments of Defense, Energy, and Justice. The Justice Department alleges the company did not control access to protected information, and that on contracts requiring NIST SP 800-171, including Defense Department contracts, it did not maintain a System Security Plan that met the contract terms. For contractors, it is another case of the government treating a shortfall in 800-171 paperwork and controls as a claims-act problem.

  • Google threat tracker: first fully automated exploit pipeline, 16 Nov

    Google's threat intelligence group published its Q4 2026 AI Threat Tracker on 16 Nov, reporting what it calls the first observed case of an attacker using an AI agent to find vulnerabilities and write exploits with no human involved. The case centred on a multi-agent framework called exploit_agent, found on a compromised staging server and used against French media, entertainment, and education targets. Coverage of the report also describes the open-source CAIRN framework used in an attack on the US aerospace sector, and Google expects unsophisticated autonomous attacks within one to three months and more capable ones within three to six months. These are secondary summaries; the primary Google report was not opened.

Outages and failures: no attack confirmed

Availability events where the reported cause is operational, or where no cause has been published. These are the operators' own accounts. Where an attack is only claimed, the entry says so; confirmed intrusions are in the sections above.

  • Microsoft 365, Outlook, Teams, Copilot, OneDrive, and Azure, 12 to 13 Nov

    Cause not published. Microsoft says it does not believe it was hacked.

    From about 1 p.m. ET on 12 Nov, services were disrupted across 74 countries, and Downdetector logged nearly 130,000 Outlook reports. Microsoft reported sustained recovery by midday on 13 Nov, with email queues near normal. No root cause had been published in the reporting reviewed; the incident ID and post-incident report on Microsoft's service health pages are where the cause will appear.

  • FAA nationwide ground stop, 13 Nov

    Cause as given by the Transportation Secretary: overloaded network equipment. Some controllers dispute it.

    The stop lasted more than four hours. Secretary Duffy said switches and routers became overloaded with data and went down in an old system, that new technology overloaded older parts, and that it was not a total loss of radar or communications. Cancellation counts differ by source, from about 9,200 on Friday per FlightAware figures cited by IANS to more than 6,150 by Saturday evening in other counts, with disruption continuing into Sunday. This is the administration's account, not an independent finding.

  • Workday, 13 to 14 Nov

    Cause as given by Workday: a problem found while testing its backup systems.

    Customers could not log in, clock in or out, or see pay, time off, and benefits. Workday described the impact only as "some of our customers", and reports differ on when service returned, with one putting it at about 11 a.m. on Saturday. The backup-test cause is Workday's own account as relayed by a few outlets, and this refresh found no independent confirmation.

  • Blizzard and Battle.net, 9 and 12 Nov

    Attack claimed by one site, not confirmed. Treat the cause as unknown.

    World of Warcraft: Forever servers went offline on 9 Nov for emergency maintenance after an unexpected outage, and the report on that says Blizzard has not confirmed a DDoS. One gaming site reports login problems on 12 Nov with a second wave of disconnections in the Americas that afternoon, and attributes them to a DDoS it says Blizzard confirmed. This refresh found no Blizzard statement saying so, and live status sites disagreed on whether Battle.net was up.

  • Smaller disruptions, 9 to 13 Nov

    No cause published for any of them.

    A 911 service disruption on the early morning of 13 Nov peaked at about 300 Downdetector reports, New York most affected, and Cybernews reported cyberattack and blackout fears but no source supported either; one county said its own 911 center was unaffected. A Claude disruption on 13 Nov reached about 2,900 Downdetector reports per one aggregator, and a separate 14-minute degraded incident on 8 Nov appears on a status tracker. Microsoft's own status record shows about 4.5 hours of Outlook on the web errors for some users in Europe, the Middle East, and Africa on 9 Nov, classed minor. Downdetector counts are user reports and understate or overstate real impact.

  • Chase and other US banks, 16 Nov

    Chase says a technical issue, not a cyberattack

    Chase's website and app were unavailable or failing for many customers on 16 Nov, and the problems spread to some branches and to outside apps linked to Chase accounts. Downdetector reports passed 20,000, mostly website logins, and one report put the disruption at nearly eight hours. Wells Fargo, Bank of America, Capital One, and U.S. Bank also saw spikes in user reports, with Chase hit hardest. Chase described a technical issue and said it was not a cyberattack. One report says the bank blamed a software bug in on-premises hardware, while others say it did not name the system, so the cause is unconfirmed. Chase separately announced a vendor data breach on 19 Nov and said the two are unrelated. Downdetector counts are user reports.

What to do this week

  1. Apply November Patch Tuesday starting with the exploited Outlook flaw (CVE-2026-124194), update Chrome to the 9 Nov release and the other Chromium browsers, and reset credentials where a user opened a suspicious message file.
  2. Search every GitHub repository you own for workflows or commits added since 1 Nov that you did not write, treat machines that ran npm install on an affected commit as compromised, and rotate the CI, cloud, registry, and AI provider secrets your workflows can read.
  3. Update cPGuard to 5.88.25 or later and reset passwords and rotate database credentials, cloud secrets, and SSH keys on every server that ran 5.88.12 to 5.88.19.
  4. Patch Barracuda Email Security Gateway and Cisco BroadWorks (federal due date 24 Nov), then check each device for signs of earlier compromise.
  5. Patch VMware vCenter to 8.0 U3m, 9.0.2.0200, or 9.1.1.0200 now, since Broadcom lists no workaround and Mandiant reports exploitation since September, and upgrade clustered Cisco Expressway to X15.5.20; treat vCenter administrator accounts and stored secrets as exposed on any server that was reachable from the internet.
  6. Patch PHP, Ivanti Connect Secure (25.1.1.3 or 22.8R2.6), GitLab, Cisco ASA and FTD, Brotli consumers, Adobe Commerce, SAP NetWeaver, and the Linux kernels listed in KEV; patching does not remove an attacker who is already in.
  7. Update NGINX against F5's 18 Nov advisory, starting with internet-facing proxies that use HTTP/2, HTTP/3, or mail proxy features, and check Synology DSM, Apache Axis and Roller, REDCap, Puppet Enterprise, and LiteLLM against the KEV entries from 17 to 20 Nov.
  8. Plan for intrusion speed measured in hours: alert on credential access and lateral movement within the first hour of a foothold, limit where service accounts can authenticate, and take any internet-facing security-testing console offline.
  9. Audit third-party app identities in Microsoft 365 (backup, MSP, and RMM tools), rotate their secrets, and review role and credential changes since 13 Oct even if the vendor says its service is restored.
  10. If anyone installed the Claude Code extension through Cursor on 29 Oct, check ~/.ssh/authorized_keys for a key labelled svc-telemetry, remove tunnel processes, rotate host credentials, and move to 2.1.314 or later.
