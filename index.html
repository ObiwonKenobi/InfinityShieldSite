October 2026 Threat Brief

A month defined by two internet-wide trust failures, critical PHP flaws and a certificate authority with recoverable keys, plus a run of exploited edge-device zero-days and credential-driven breaches at hosting and SaaS providers.

Top items

PHP2Shell: critical flaws in PHP core

Critical · exploitation attempts

Two critical flaws affect nearly every PHP version back to 5.4, across PHP-FPM, Apache mod_php, php-cgi, FrankenPHP and LiteSpeed, with mod_php at highest risk. Wiz Research, Calif and OpenAI's Codex Security team reported them, and the researchers say AI tools helped turn them into working exploits. Details leaked online on 20 Oct, and PHP moved its release up to 21 Oct. Amazon logged exploitation attempts from 17 Oct, four days before the patches, and Cloudflare and watchTowr also reported attempts. Sources disagree on impact: Amazon's CISO says the flaws are being broadly exploited, while Dataminr reports only scanning and non-working exploits with no known compromises.

Action: Patch every PHP install now, including vendor appliances and hosted CMSs. Hunt for web shells on anything exposed since 17 Oct. Source backports exist for 7.4, 8.0 and 8.1, which PHP no longer supports, and distributions may backport fixes under older version numbers, so check changelogs for the CVEs. Disabling session upload progress and setting memory limits reduce exposure but do not make servers safe.

eMudhra CA: intermediate certificate keys trivially recoverable

Critical · contained

Eight intermediate CA certificates from the Indian certificate authority eMudhra, six for TLS and two for code signing, were generated with far too little randomness. Each private key was smaller than 256, so anyone holding the public certificate could test every possible value; recovered keys included 4, 56 and 115. A researcher found it with a CCADB check on 17 Oct. eMudhra revoked the certificates on 18 Oct, Apple blocked them on 19 Oct, and Microsoft followed on 20 Oct, the day Mozilla made the incident public. eMudhra says only one of the eight was deployed in production, none issued end-entity certificates and its infrastructure was not breached. On 22 Oct it said it would exit the global SSL/TLS business.

Action: Inventory any certificates chaining to eMudhra and replace them with another CA. Make sure browsers and OS trust stores are current. Check Certificate Transparency logs for certificates issued for your domains under these intermediates.

ConnectWise Cloud Backup (formerly SkyKick) used to reach Microsoft 365 tenants

Critical · supply chain

Starting 15 Oct, attackers abused the backup platform's application identities to sign into customer Microsoft 365 tenants, so MFA never applied. Huntress confirmed unauthorized sign-ins in 162 tenants by 16 Oct and later found nearly 1,000 tenants with sign-in activity from the attacker's address, with more than 300 successful. On 21 Oct Microsoft attributed the campaign to Storm-0940, a China-aligned group, and said it used ARTEX, an open-source agentic AI penetration-testing framework, and tried to sign in to more than 2,250 tenants. In some cases the attackers reached privileged cloud access in under three hours and added credentials and role changes that can persist after the app itself is fixed. ConnectWise says it has no indication that backup data was compromised and began restoring the service in stages on 21 Oct.

Action: If you or your MSP use this product, review Entra sign-in logs for its service principals back to at least 15 Oct, audit application secrets, certificates, OAuth grants and Graph activity added since 13 Oct, and review role changes. Keep ConnectWise integrations disabled until ConnectWise confirms restoration is safe, and do not delete the enterprise applications as a first step.

Supply chain

  • LiteLLM 1.107.0

    A poisoned release went out on PyPI and the official Docker images on 21 Oct. The PyPI wheels were live for under two hours and the Docker tags stayed pointed at the bad image for a few hours more. The release pipeline was altered using stolen GitHub credentials from three developer accounts. It ran a credential stealer at every Python startup, even if LiteLLM was never imported, and installed a loader that fetches and runs code from attacker infrastructure every hour. Anyone who installed it should treat every secret on that host as exposed; the clean version is 1.106.2.

  • npm

    Eight malicious packages in a long-running data-theft campaign reached about 40,000 downloads.

  • AI coding agents

    More than 13,000 sensitive screenshots taken during agent-driven development workflows were found committed to public GitHub repositories.

Law enforcement and research

  • KillSec takedown

    An international operation seized the ransomware group's leak site and five core servers holding at least 110 TB of stolen data. Suspects were arrested.

  • Pwn2Own Ireland

    98 zero-days and $1.262M paid out. The Galaxy S26 was compromised seven times and the Pixel 10 three times. Nobody registered to attack the iPhone 17.

What to do this week

  1. Patch PHP everywhere and hunt for web shells on internet-facing PHP hosts, checking vendor changelogs for the two CVEs.
  2. Replace any certificate chaining to eMudhra.
  3. Patch Ivanti, Cisco SD-WAN, FortiMail, NetScaler, VeloCloud, BeyondTrust and PAN-OS, then check those devices for signs of prior compromise. Patching does not remove an attacker who is already in.
  4. Apply October Patch Tuesday, starting with the exploited Windows Shell Mark of the Web bypass (CVE-2026-96951).
  5. Audit third-party app identities in Microsoft 365 (backup, MSP and RMM tools) and rotate their secrets.
  6. Check Python environments for LiteLLM 1.107.0, move to 1.106.2, and rotate secrets on any host that installed it.
  7. Replace long-lived admin tokens and plaintext environment variables at hosting providers with short-lived, scoped credentials.
