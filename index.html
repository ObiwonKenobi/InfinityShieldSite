October 2026 Threat Brief

A month defined by two internet-wide trust failures, a PHP remote-code-execution pair and a certificate authority with guessable keys, plus a run of exploited edge-device zero-days and credential-driven breaches at hosting and SaaS providers.

Top items

PHP2Shell: unauthenticated RCE in PHP core

Critical · exploited

Two memory-safety flaws in PHP 5.4 through 8.5 let a remote, unauthenticated attacker run code on affected web servers. The bugs were found with AI assistance in August. Details leaked on Hacker News on 20 Oct, a day before advisories were published on 21 Oct. Amazon saw an exploit attempt on 17 Oct, four days before disclosure, and watchTowr and Cloudflare confirmed in-the-wild exploitation by 21 Oct.

eMudhra CA: intermediate certificate keys trivially recoverable

Critical · contained

Eight intermediate CA certificates from the Indian certificate authority eMudhra used P-384 private keys with 3 to 8 bits of entropy. One key was literally the number 4. Anyone holding the public certificate could derive the key in under a second and issue trusted HTTPS certificates for any domain. A researcher found it via a CCADB check on 17 Oct. eMudhra revoked the certificates on 18 Oct, and Chrome, Firefox, Apple and Microsoft all blocked them by 20 Oct. On 22 Oct eMudhra said it would exit the global SSL/TLS business.

Action: Inventory any certificates chaining to eMudhra and replace them with another CA. Make sure browsers and OS trust stores are current. Check Certificate Transparency logs for certificates issued for your domains under these intermediates.

ConnectWise Cloud Backup (formerly SkyKick) used to reach Microsoft 365 tenants

Critical · supply chain

Starting 15 Oct, attackers abused the backup platform's application identities to sign into customer Microsoft 365 tenants. Nearly 1,000 Entra tenants were targeted, with 300+ successful sign-ins and at least 162 tenants confirmed compromised in early counts. Reports blaming a China-linked actor using AI agents are not supported by primary sources so far.

Action: If you or your MSP use this product, review Entra sign-in logs for its service principals since 15 Oct, rotate its credentials and consent grants, and audit mailbox and SharePoint access.

Supply chain

  • LiteLLM 1.107.0

    A poisoned release went out on PyPI and the official Docker images on 21 Oct and stayed up about two hours. It ran a credential stealer at Python startup and installed a remote-access backdoor. Attackers published it with stolen maintainer GitHub tokens. Anyone who installed it should treat every secret on that host as exposed.

  • npm

    Eight malicious packages in a long-running data-theft campaign reached about 40,000 downloads.

  • AI coding agents

    More than 13,000 sensitive screenshots taken during agent-driven development workflows were found committed to public GitHub repositories.

Law enforcement and research

  • KillSec takedown

    An international operation seized the ransomware group's leak site and five core servers holding at least 110 TB of stolen data. Suspects were arrested.

  • Pwn2Own Ireland

    98 zero-days and $1.26M paid out over 6–8 Oct. The Pixel 10 and Galaxy S26 were each compromised four times. Nobody attempted the iPhone 17.

What to do this week

  1. Patch PHP everywhere and hunt for web shells on internet-facing PHP hosts.
  2. Replace any certificate chaining to eMudhra.
  3. Patch Ivanti, Cisco SD-WAN, FortiMail, NetScaler, VeloCloud, BeyondTrust and PAN-OS, then check those devices for signs of prior compromise. Patching does not remove an attacker who is already in.
  4. Apply October Patch Tuesday, prioritising Outlook, Exchange and Windows Shell.
  5. Audit third-party app identities in Microsoft 365 (backup, MSP and RMM tools) and rotate their secrets.
  6. Check Python environments for LiteLLM 1.107.0 and rotate secrets on any host that installed it.
  7. Replace long-lived admin tokens and plaintext environment variables at hosting providers with short-lived, scoped credentials.
