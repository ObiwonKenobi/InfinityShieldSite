# Rolling 30 day threat brief: weekly procedure

This file is the instruction set for the Monday refresh of the rolling brief at
https://www.infinityshield.co/brief/. A scheduled Claude Code session reads it
and follows it. Edit this file to change how the brief is produced; the
schedule itself lives in the account's scheduled tasks, not in the repo.

The monthly archive (archive/) is a separate product with its own schedule and
is never edited by the weekly run.

## What the weekly run produces

- `brief/data/current.json` rewritten for the new 30 day window
- `brief/index.html` and the period and lede on `index.html`, both regenerated by
  `python3 tools/build_brief.py` (never hand edit these two files)
- a commit pushed straight to `main`, which publishes the brief

The owner reads the brief on the live site; there is no review step. That makes
steps 6 and 7 the only gate, so never skip or weaken them, and never push if
either fails.

## The window

The window is the 30 days ending on the Sunday before the run. For a run on
Monday 2 Nov 2026 the window is 3 Oct to 1 Nov 2026 and the period string is
`3 Oct – 1 Nov 2026` (digits, three letter month, en dash with spaces, year
once at the end, or on both dates when the window spans a year boundary:
`29 Dec 2026 – 27 Jan 2027`). `issued` is the run date in the same style,
`2 Nov 2026`.

## Procedure

1. Attach the repo with the `add_repo` tool (owner `ObiwonKenobi`, repo
   `InfinityShieldSite`, access `push`) if it is not already available, then
   clone it once with `git clone --depth 50` and a generous timeout. Do not
   clone twice in parallel. Work on `main`, starting from `origin/main`.
2. Copy `brief/data/current.json` to `/tmp/previous-brief.json` before changing it; step 6
   needs it for the "New this week" section. Then read it. It is the previous week's brief and
   your starting point. Read one or two files in `archive/data/` for voice and
   format. Read `tools/build_brief.py` for the validation rules.
3. Carry forward, then update. Drop every item whose main event or disclosure
   is now older than the window. Keep items still inside the window, and update
   their status where it changed (patch shipped, added to CISA KEV, victim
   count revised, arrest made). Then research the days that are new since last
   week, usually the last 7 to 9 days.
4. Research with WebSearch and WebFetch. Run 10 to 16 searches covering:
   critical and exploited vulnerabilities and zero-days (CISA KEV additions,
   vendor advisories, the Microsoft Patch Tuesday if one fell in the window),
   supply chain attacks (npm, PyPI, GitHub Actions, CI/CD, MSP and RMM tools),
   data breaches with record counts, ransomware and law enforcement actions,
   notable research disclosures, and certificate or internet-wide trust
   failures. Prefer primary sources: the vendor advisory, CISA, the regulator or
   court filing, the victim's own notice. Reputable security press is fine as a
   second source.
   Primary-source sweep every week, before the general searches (all hosts are on the
   environment allowlist; if one is blocked, say so in the summary and carry on):
   - SEC EDGAR (`www.sec.gov`): 8-K filings in the window under Item 1.05 (material
     cybersecurity incident) and Item 8.01 where the text describes a cyber incident. Take the
     filing date, what the company says was affected, and whether it calls the impact material.
   - Breach notification listings: HHS OCR (`ocrportal.hhs.gov`) and the Maine, California, and
     Texas attorney general sites (`www.maineattorneygeneral.gov`, `oag.ca.gov`,
     `www.texasattorneygeneral.gov`). Use them to replace estimates with filed counts; a filed
     count goes in `records`, and a difference from the press figure goes in the text.
   - Law enforcement and sanctions: DOJ (`www.justice.gov`) and Treasury (`home.treasury.gov`)
     press releases, plus FBI IC3 PSAs (`www.ic3.gov`) and CISA advisories (`www.cisa.gov`).
   - Non-US view: JPCERT/CC (`www.jpcert.or.jp`), NCSC UK (`www.ncsc.gov.uk`), CERT-FR
     (`www.cert.ssi.gouv.fr`), CCCS (`www.cyber.gc.ca`), ACSC (`www.cyber.gov.au`), and CERT-EU
     (`cert.europa.eu`). Include an item only if the agency itself or a named victim confirms it.
   - Note in the final summary which sweep sources produced something new and which were empty
     or unreachable, so a quiet week is distinguishable from a blocked source.
   Standing checks every week, in addition to the general searches:
   - Federal agencies and the Defense Industrial Base: DHS, DoD, USDA, GSA,
     and FedRAMP-authorized providers, including DDoS claims against federal
     sites (hacktivist claims alone go in `unverified`).
   - JAMIS Prime ERP outage: it sits in `unverified` on one secondary source.
     Move it into the brief only if JAMIS or reputable press confirms cause and
     scope; drop it once it is older than the window with no confirmation.
5. Write the new `brief/data/current.json`. Schema and limits are below.
6. Run `python3 tools/build_brief.py --check` and fix every problem it lists.
   Then run `python3 tools/build_brief.py --since /tmp/previous-brief.json`. It compares the new
   brief with last week's, writes a `changes` block and an `added` date on each item into
   `brief/data/current.json`, then writes `brief/index.html` and updates `index.html`. Read the
   printed counts. Edit the `changes` block by hand only to improve wording: replace "Left the
   brief" with the real reason (aged out, merged into another item, superseded) and tighten any
   "text revised" into what actually changed. Later builds in the same run use no flag. If you
   skip `--since`, `--check` fails because the old `changes` block is from an earlier run.
7. Confirm the only changed files are `brief/data/current.json`,
   `brief/index.html`, and `index.html`. Confirm `grep -c "—" brief/ index.html`
   reports zero em dashes.
8. Commit with the message `Rolling brief: refresh to <period>`, then
   `git fetch origin main && git rebase origin/main && git push origin HEAD:main`.
   If the rebase conflicts, stop and report; do not force-push. Do not open a
   pull request.
9. Finish with a short summary for the owner: items added, dropped, and
   changed, everything in `unverified`, and any weak spots or single-source
   claims, plus the commit hash.

## Outages versus attacks

Availability events go in `outages`, never in `other`, `supply`, or `breaches`, unless an
attack is confirmed. Confirmed means the operator, a government agency, or a named security
firm says it was an attack. An attack claimed only by a hacktivist group, a gaming site, or
a single aggregator stays in `outages` with `basis` saying "attack claimed, not confirmed".
`basis` is one line giving the cause as the operator or agency reports it, or "cause not
published". If a cause is later confirmed as an attack, move the entry to the right
intrusion section the next week. Downdetector counts are user reports and are labelled so.

## Dates for the timeline

Every item in `vulns`, `supply`, `breaches`, `other`, and `outages` carries `when`, an ISO date
(`2026-11-10`): the day the item became public or was confirmed (KEV addition, vendor fix,
disclosure or announcement, the day an outage began). Add `to` (also ISO) only for an event that
ran continuously across days, such as a two day outage; do not use it to bridge separate
events. The build draws these on the Timeline section as a day by day grid per category, so a
wrong date puts an item in the wrong cluster. A `when` before the window start is accepted for
an item that began earlier and is still active; it is drawn on the first day. When carrying an
item forward, keep its `when` unless the event itself changed. The `top` items are not plotted.

`when` is the reporting or confirmation date, which is what the Timeline plots. When the activity
began earlier than `when` (an intrusion months before disclosure, exploitation before a patch),
the item's text must say when it began. The "month by month" view reads `archive/data/*.json`
for the months already archived and uses this brief only for days after the last archived month,
so archive items should carry `when` too (the monthly run adds it; items with no supportable date
are left without one and are counted as not plotted).

## Rules

- Never invent a CVE number, a record count, a date, a victim name, or a URL.
  Every URL in `sources` must come from your own search or fetch results.
- Where sources conflict, say so in the text and give both figures.
- A claim from a single weak source goes in `unverified`, not in the tables.
- If something cannot be verified, leave it out. A shorter brief is fine.
- Dry and factual. Short paragraphs, plain numbers, no adjectives doing the
  work of facts, no marketing language, no exclamation marks.
- No em dashes anywhere. Use a comma, a colon, or a full stop. En dashes are
  only for the date range in `period`.
- Dates inside text are `20 Oct`, with the year only when it is not the
  current year.
- Do not edit `archive/`, `about.html`, `brief.css`, or anything under `tools/`.
- Do not include a model name in the commit message.

## Schema for brief/data/current.json

```json
{
  "period":   "29 Sep – 28 Oct 2026",
  "issued":   "28 Oct 2026",
  "lede":     "Two sentences at most. What defined the window.",
  "top": [
    {
      "title":  "Short headline",
      "sev":    "crit | high | med",
      "status": "Critical · exploited",
      "body":   "One paragraph. What happened, who reported it, what is confirmed, where sources disagree.",
      "facts":  [["CVE", "CVE-2026-00000"], ["CVSS", "9.8"], ["Fixed in", "1.2.3"]],
      "action": "Imperative. What a defender should do this week."
    }
  ],
  "vulns": [
    {"product": "Vendor Product", "cve": "CVE-2026-00000", "allows": "What an attacker gets", "sev": "crit", "status": "Exploited", "note": "KEV 20 Oct. Fixed in 1.2.3.", "when": "2026-10-20"}
  ],
  "supply":   [{"label": "Package or service, date", "text": "One paragraph.", "when": "2026-10-20"}],
  "breaches": [{"org": "Organization", "scale": "4.2M records", "records": 4200000, "cause": "How, when, what data.", "when": "2026-10-20"}],
  "unverified": [{"item": "Short name of the claim", "why": "One line, 30 words at most, on why it is unverified"}],
  "other":    [{"label": "Event, date", "text": "One paragraph.", "when": "2026-10-20"}],
  "outages":  [{"label": "Service, dates", "basis": "Reported cause in one line", "text": "One paragraph.", "when": "2026-11-12", "to": "2026-11-13"}],
  "todo":     ["Imperative sentence.", "Another."],
  "sources":  [{"title": "Publisher: headline", "url": "https://..."}]
}
```

Limits enforced by `build_brief.py --check`: `top` exactly 3; `vulns` 6 to 16;
`supply` 1 to 6; `breaches` 6 to 15; `other` 1 to 6; `outages` 0 to 6; `todo` 5 to 10;
`sources` 10 to 45. `facts` and `note` are optional. `sev` must be `crit`, `high`, or
`med`.

`records` is required on every breach: the number of people or records
affected as a plain integer (`20000000` for "~20M patients"), or `null` when no
count was disclosed or the figure counts something else (servers, companies).
When a source gives a range or two figures, use the number of people if there is
one, otherwise the larger confirmed figure, and keep the nuance in `scale`. The
build sums these for the "people or records exposed" figure and charts them, so
a wrong order of magnitude is very visible.

## CISA KEV data

The build refreshes `brief/data/kev.json` from the public CISA KEV feed and
draws the "CISA Known Exploited Vulnerabilities" section from it: additions in
the window, those linked to ransomware, federal due dates in the next 14 days,
and which of this brief's CVEs are in the catalog. If the feed is unreachable
(the environment's network policy must allow `www.cisa.gov`), the build keeps
the previous snapshot and prints a notice; the page then shows the snapshot's
date. Mention a stale snapshot in your final summary. Never edit
`kev.json` by hand.
